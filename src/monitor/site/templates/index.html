3 · Market state

The fragility index measures the size of the pile of dry material, not the timing of the spark: levered longs paying to hold, no protection bought, price at the highs, no new stablecoin money arriving.

4 · Active triggers

Pre-committed responses to configurations known to precede forced flow. Thresholds are frozen; hit rates come with their (small) sample sizes.

5 · Trade structures

Where durable return in crypto funds has come from: basis, funding carry, volatility premium, unlock shorts. Gross, cost, net, and the venue whose failure would matter most.

6 · Event strip · next four weeks

Scheduled supply and dates that move positioning: cliffs in days of real volume, governance, upgrades, large options expiries.

7 · Tier 2/3 screens and liquidity gate

Screens condition attention; they are not forecasts. Each carries its trailing information coefficient with a standard error of the same order.

8 · Exchange tokens

The one sector with equity-like cash flows: fee revenue, a shareholder yield from burns, an idiosyncratic tail (exchange solvency) that is orthogonal to BTC, and a market-priced solvency signal in the token itself. Ranking here is within one sector with real fundamentals. On-chain fees are verified; CEX revenue is estimated and flagged.

9 · Resistance / support tests

Φ is not a direction forecaster. This panel estimates two separate things and multiplies them: the probability of a clean break vs a rejection at a test, and the size of the move each way. The asymmetry lives in the size — a rejection can be rare and still dominate because its move is large. The base rate is measured on full price history (the anchor); the crowding/Φ overlay is preliminary on a short leverage sample and is never presented as established below the sample floor. Every probability is conditional on the level definition and whether the daily close has cleared, and carries its event count.
